Why Brutalism Is Awesome

2025Architecture

Most people look at a brutalist building and see an ugly concrete box. I get it. I used to think that too. But the more of them I look at, the more I think brutalism is one of the most honest styles of architecture there is.

It doesn't pretend

The name doesn't come from "brutal." It comes from béton brut, French for raw concrete. A brutalist building shows you what it's made of and how it stands up. No fake columns, no cladding hiding the structure. You can often still see the grain of the wooden forms the concrete was poured into. What you see is what's actually there.

It was built for people

A lot of it came out of rebuilding after World War II, when cities needed housing, schools and public buildings, fast and at scale. The Barbican in London, the photo at the top of my site, went up on a bombed-out site. It's a whole neighborhood in one design: flats, a school, an arts center, gardens, a lake. It was an attempt to design how people live, not just the buildings they live in.

It gets better with age

Plants love concrete. Give the Barbican's balcony planters a few decades and the gray starts to disappear behind green. That contrast, hard concrete and soft plants, is my favorite thing in architecture.

Why I care

I think good software works the same way. Show the structure. Don't hide the parts that hold it up behind decoration. Build something useful first and let it earn its character over time. That's how I try to build Hermie.
