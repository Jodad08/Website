Notes on Stanford's entrepreneurial ecosystem

2024Notes

Stanford produces a strange number of companies. These are my notes on why, and which parts of it you can copy without being there.

What's there

  • StartX. A nonprofit accelerator for Stanford founders that doesn't take equity. It lowers the bar to trying.
  • The d.school. Stanford's design institute teaches people to prototype fast and talk to users early, which is most of what building a startup is.
  • BASES. A student entrepreneurship group that runs events and competitions, so starting a company is a normal thing to do, not a weird one.
  • Tech licensing. Stanford's licensing office helps research leave the lab and become a company. Google's early search technology was licensed this way.
  • Sand Hill Road. A lot of venture capital sits a few minutes from campus. Getting a meeting is a drive, not a flight.

What actually matters

None of those things alone explains it. The real advantage is density. When the people around you are starting companies, it stops feeling like a risky exception and starts feeling like the default. Advice, cofounders and first customers are all a conversation away.

What you can copy

  • Put yourself around people who are building things, online or in person.
  • Talk to users before you write code.
  • Ask for help early. Most people say yes.
  • Treat starting something as normal. It is.
